# Dioramas v3: three contest scenes for the Pixoo 64

Goal: replace the current scenes (nave, trench, maglev) with three polished, imaginative 64x64 animated
dioramas for the Devoxx Belgium 2026 Google Cloud booth contest (a developer conference). Submissions are
judged automatically on the rubric below, then by Gemini Vision for craft and originality.

Repo: `C:\Users\guill\Documents\git\lumen-grid` (Python, NumPy, Pillow, pytest). Branch `dioramas-v3`.

## Global Constraints

These bind every task.

### Format

- Every scene module lives in `ledviz/scenes/<name>.py`. It exposes `N = 30` and
  `frame(i: int, n: int = N) -> np.ndarray`, which returns a `(64, 64, 3)` uint8 frame.
- The GIF is 30 frames at 10 fps. The Pixoo 64 replays only about the first 30 to 32 frames.

### Seamless loop

- `frame()` must NOT wrap `i` (no `i %= n`). Every motion must be periodic in N by construction, and
  `frame(N)` must equal `frame(0)` exactly.
- Watch for drift terms like `i // 2` or `(y0 + 2 * i) // 15`: they are not periodic in 30.

### Pixel craft

- Motion moves in whole-pixel steps only.
- At most 64 unique RGB colours across all frames. No dithering. No smooth gradients or alpha
  blending that creates new colours.
- Build the art as hand-placed character pixel maps, one character per palette entry, stamped with
  `ledviz.core.stamp`. Procedural code is only for motion, flicker, colour cycling and simple
  geometry (an ellipse for a porthole, a straight beam).

### Contest rubric proxies

Each scene must pass every proxy, measured over all 30 frames:

| Proxy | Requirement |
|---|---|
| True black | at least 0.45 of pixels are exactly (0,0,0) |
| Dim pixels | at most 0.05 of pixels have a max channel between 1 and 23 (a dim LED wastes contrast) |
| Saturation | mean HSV saturation of lit pixels (max channel ≥ 24) at least 0.70 |
| Vivid | at least 0.10 of pixels have saturation > 0.6 and max channel > 120 |
| Peak | 95th-percentile max channel of lit pixels at least 230 |
| Motion | mean absolute frame-to-frame difference at least 3.0, and no two consecutive frames identical |

### Art direction (all scenes)

- **Clear subject.** One readable focal subject that Gemini Vision can name at a glance.
- **Big connected colour shapes.** The scorer ignores isolated single-pixel specks and rewards
  spatially connected colour clusters.
- **Story beats.** Several distinct animated beats per loop; small micro-jitter alone is penalised.
- **Imaginative.** Each scene should feel original, not a stock backdrop.
- **Verify by looking.** Render a contact sheet with `python preview.py <name>` (Task 1) and look at it
  with your image-reading tool before claiming done. Judge silhouettes, readability and clutter at
  1:1 pixel scale, not only the numbers.

### Code

- Match the style of the existing scenes: a module docstring listing the layers, a `PAL` dict, ASCII
  maps as module constants, small private helpers and short comments.
- Commit on branch `dioramas-v3`.
- Commit messages: plain text, no backticks, and NO Co-Authored-By or any other trailer.

## Task 1: Rubric gate and preview tool

Add the rubric proxies as a real test gate, plus a contact-sheet preview for visual review.

1. **`ledviz/rubric.py`.** Add `metrics(frames: np.ndarray) -> dict` computing every proxy in the
   Global Constraints table over a `(n, 64, 64, 3)` array: `black`, `dim`, `saturation`, `vivid`,
   `peak`, `motion`, `min_step` (the smallest mean step between consecutive frames) and `colours`.
   Also add a `THRESHOLDS` mapping holding the table's values.
2. **`tests/test_rubric.py`.** For every scene in `EFFECTS`, assert each proxy passes, with a
   failure message naming the proxy, its value and its threshold. Mutation-prove the gate by hand:
   temporarily brighten the background of one scene and confirm `black` fails; swap in a static frame
   and confirm `motion` fails. Record the proof in the report. Do not commit the mutations.
3. **`preview.py`** (repo root). `python preview.py <name> [--out PATH]` writes a PNG contact sheet:
   all 30 frames at 4x nearest-neighbour in a 6x5 grid with a 2 px black gutter. The default path is
   `out/<name>_sheet.png`. It also prints the metrics table for that scene. Add `out/*_sheet.png`
   to `.gitignore`.
4. **`ledviz/effects.py`.** Register `neon` and `titan` alongside the existing scenes, so the
   gates run on them. They will fail the seam test and maybe others: that is expected and is fixed in
   Tasks 2 and 3. Mark those two scenes' failures with `pytest.mark.xfail(strict=True)` through a
   module-level `PENDING = {"neon", "titan"}` set in the tests, so the suite is green and turns red
   once a scene starts passing while still listed. Tasks 2 and 3 remove their names from `PENDING`.
   Leave nave, trench and maglev registered; Task 5 removes them. Where nave, trench or maglev fail
   the rubric gate, add them to `PENDING` as well and note it in the report.

## Task 2: Neon Rain (cyberpunk), polish to final

`ledviz/scenes/neon.py` exists as a draft. Read it fully first, then render its contact sheet.

**Concept.** The camera follows a lone walker with a glowing LED umbrella down a rain-soaked neon
street at night. The wet street mirrors everything.

**What exists.**
- Layers: a true-black sky; far towers drawn as outlines on black (1 px/frame, 30 px tile);
  shopfronts (2 px/frame, 60 px tile) with a magenta vertical glyph sign, a doorway glow, a ramen bowl
  sign with steam, a noodle bar window with two diners, a waving neon cat and a vending machine.
- Screen-space elements: a holographic koi, a police drone, rain, and the walker with a walk cycle
  and a colour chase around the umbrella rim.
- A rippling street reflection snapped to a small palette, and rain splashes.

**Known defects to fix.**
1. **Seam.** `frame(30) != frame(0)`. The steam uses `i // 2`; find and fix every non-periodic term.
2. **The walker is small and gets lost against the shopfronts.** Make the walker the clear hero:
   - Make the walker slightly larger, with a crisp silhouette that separates from what is behind it:
     a dark keyline, or a pool of umbrella light on the ground under them.
   - Make the umbrella the most striking object in the frame.
   - Keep the walk cycle readable.
3. **Shopfront clutter.** The steam pixels and chopsticks around the ramen bowl read as noise. Make
   each sign an iconic, clean neon pictogram.
4. **The koi** must read as a fish at a glance: a clear head, eye and tail.
5. **Story beats.** Check the loop has distinct beats: the koi pass, the drone strobe, the cat's
   wave, a sign flicker, and the splashes. Add one delightful developer-conference touch that fits
   the world. Example: one shop sign is a neon `</>` or `{ }`, or the vending machine sells "COFFEE".
   Keep it legible at 64 px.

**Keep.** True-black walls, the reflection idea, and integer parallax speeds that loop: 1 and
2 px/frame with 30 and 60 px tiles.

**Done when.**
- All gates pass and `neon` is removed from `PENDING`.
- The contact sheet has been inspected and every defect above is resolved.

## Task 3: Warlord Titan (Warhammer 40k), redesign to final

`ledviz/scenes/titan.py` exists as a rough draft. Its layer structure, fire columns, embers,
turbo-laser beam, explosion and mega-bolter are usable. **The Titan itself does not read.** It looks
like a red blob on stilts: the head is hidden behind the bolter arm, the legs are thin poles, and there
is no recognisable Titan silhouette. Redraw it.

**Concept.** A Warlord Titan, a god-machine of the Adeptus Titanicus, strides through a burning hive
city at night. The camera tracks it.

**Titan art direction.** Fill most of the frame height, roughly 38 to 44 px tall:
- **Carapace.** A massive hunched carapace with crenellated battlements, or a gothic roof line with
  little spires on top, and gold trim.
- **Head.** Forward and low on the chest, with a brooding brow and a glowing green eye slit, clearly
  visible and never hidden by an arm.
- **Arms.** Two huge weapon arms hanging forward of the body. The far arm, a long turbo-laser, sits
  behind the torso. The near arm, a mega-bolter with multiple barrels, sits in front but lower than
  the head.
- **Legs.** Thick armoured legs with big knee plates, armoured shins and broad feet. Digitigrade or
  pillar style; the walk must read as weight.
- **Detail.** A hanging banner with a skull-cog between the legs. A pulsing plasma reactor at the back.
- **Colour.** Blood-red armour (Legio Mortis), gold trim, blue-steel guns, a green eye, blue plasma.
- **Stride.** A cycle with weight: the planted foot stays planted relative to the ground scrolling
  under it at 2 px/frame where possible, the other foot lifts and swings, and the body bobs. The
  cycle's period must divide 30.

**Background.** Keep the black hive towers rim-lit by fire, but make them more gothic and more
varied: spires, broken arches, a cathedral silhouette. Fire columns and windows flicker. A red
horizon glow is allowed but keep the true-black share high.

**Beats.**
- The turbo-laser fires twice per loop and the struck building erupts.
- Mega-bolter bursts come between the shots.
- Embers rise, and the reactor pulses.
- Optionally, small fleeing figures or a tank the Titan passes, for scale.

**Fix the seam.** The ember x-drift `(y0 + 2 * i) // 15` is not periodic.

**Done when.**
- All gates pass and `titan` is removed from `PENDING`.
- The contact sheet shows an unmistakable Titan, judged by looking at the sheet.

## Task 4: Robot AI coder (new scene)

Create `ledviz/scenes/coder.py` and register it as `coder`.

**Concept.** "Pair programming with a robot" for a developer conference: a charming robot coding at
a desk at night. It must be clearly a robot, clearly coding and clearly delightful. Composition and
details are yours to imagine; quality and originality matter most.

**Ingredients to consider.** You are not bound to all of them, but the result must be at least this
rich.
- **The robot.** A rounded robot at a desk, seen in profile or three-quarter view. Its face is a
  visor screen with pixel eyes that blink and change expression, and it types with alternating hands.
  An antenna light blinks.
- **The monitor.** A big monitor showing syntax-highlighted code that scrolls: lines of coloured
  token blocks with indentation, a magenta keyword, a cyan identifier, a yellow string, a green
  comment, and a blinking cursor. Code lines can scroll 1 px/frame with a 30-row code tile so it loops.
- **The payoff beat once per loop.** For example, a test run turns the screen green "PASS ✓" (or
  "BUILD OK") and the robot's eyes become happy arcs (^ ^). Or a rubber duck on the desk quacks a
  speech bubble. Make it legible at 64 px with a 3x5 font.
- **Room details.**
  - An RGB keyboard with a colour chase.
  - A coffee mug with rising steam (the robot drinks oil: a tiny "OIL" label).
  - A server rack with blinking LEDs.
  - A window onto the night city or a starry sky.
  - Cables.
- **Background.** True black; the scene is lit by the monitor glow and the neon.

**Constraints.** Every global constraint and rubric gate applies; this scene starts outside `PENDING`
and must pass from its first commit. A static camera is allowed. If there is no camera move, the
motion beats must carry the loop; a gentle parallax is optional.

**Done when.**
- All gates pass and the contact sheet has been inspected.
- The robot and the code read clearly at 1:1.
